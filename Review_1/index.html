SYSTEM CHARACTERIZATION ENVIRONMENT TAXONOMY & MULTI-AGENT INTERACTION PROTOCOLS

Russell & Norvig's 7 Canonical Dimensions & CNP Bidding

Formal environment classification, internal agent decision architecture, and FIPA-ACL negotiation protocols.

Dimension Classification Rigorous Technical Justification & Algorithmic Impact
1. Observability Partially Observable Ambulance agents have localized field of view; cannot observe distant traffic jams, internal patient bleeding, or sudden hospital bed status changes until sensor telemetry pings arrive.
2. Agents Multi-Agent (Coop & Comp) Competitive bidding during Contract Net auctions for optimal task assignment; fully cooperative toward city-wide life-saving and preventing hospital offload delays.
3. Determinism Stochastic Emergency calls arrive following a Poisson process; traffic congestion dynamics and hospital service times follow non-deterministic probability distributions.
4. Episodicity Sequential An ambulance dispatch decision immediately impacts future spatial fleet coverage, residual fuel reserves, and downstream emergency department queue lengths.
5. Dynamism Dynamic City traffic conditions, patient clinical vitals, and hospital bed availability change continuously while the agent computes routing policies.
6. Discreteness Continuous Ambulance spatial coordinates $(x, y)$, vehicle velocities, edge costs, and biometric time series (ECG waveform) operate continuously.
7. Knownness Known Rules / Unknown State Road network topology and traffic laws are known; exact vehicle positions and emergency incident timings are discovered at runtime.

🤖 Agent Typology & Decision Architectures

🚑 AMBULANCE AGENT Utility-Based Agent (BDI)

Maximizes multi-attribute utility: $U(a) = -(w_1 \cdot \text{ETA} + w_2 \cdot \text{Distance} + w_3 \cdot \text{PatientSeverity} + w_4 \cdot \text{HospitalWait})$. Trades off transit latency against destination clinical capabilities.

📡 DISPATCHER AGENT Model-Based Goal Agent

Maintains global world model tracking fleet distribution across city quadrants. Predicts spatial coverage voids and triggers proactive staging relocations.

🏥 HOSPITAL ER AGENT Model-Based Utility Agent

Maintains internal bed queuing model. Dynamically inflates admission bid costs as occupancy exceeds $85\%$ to deter dangerous hospital overcrowding.

🤝 Contract Net Protocol (CNP) 4-Phase Flow

1
CFP Broadcast: Dispatcher receives 911 call, categorizes ESI level, and broadcasts Call for Proposals (CFP) with coordinates and clinical needs.
2
Autonomous Bidding: Standby ambulances compute marginal cost: $\text{Bid}_i = \alpha T_{\text{travel}} + \beta T_{\text{service}} - \gamma \text{Readiness}$ via Time-Dependent A* on live traffic.
3
Contract Award: Dispatcher evaluates bids against global objective function, awards contract to optimal unit, and sends rejections to other units.
4
Bilateral Hospital Bed Lock: Assigned ambulance executes handshake with candidate ER, locking trauma bed reservation before departure.
Standard: FIPA-ACL Performatives (CFP, PROPOSE, ACCEPT-PROPOSAL, INFORM)
MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATION ALGORITHMIC MODELING, OPTIMAL SEARCH & MATCHING THEORY

Time-Dependent A*, D* Lite Replanning & Gale-Shapley Matching

Formal search formulation, mathematical admissibility proof, dynamic replanning, and two-sided stable matching.

OPTIMAL SEARCH ENGINE

🧭 Time-Dependent A* (TDA*) Algorithm

$$f(n, t) = g(n, t) + h(n)$$
  • Road Graph Model: Directed graph $G = (V, E)$ where edges have time-varying traversal costs $c(u, v, t)$ based on arrival time $t$.
  • $g(n, t)$: Accumulated travel time from dispatch origin to intersection $n$.
  • $h(n)$: Straight-line Euclidean distance heuristic to destination.
📐 MATHEMATICAL PROOF OF ADMISSIBILITY & OPTIMALITY
$$h(n) = \frac{\text{EuclideanDistance}(n, \text{Goal})}{v_{\max}}$$

Let $h^*(n)$ denote the true shortest travel time from $n$ to Goal. Since vehicle speed on any road edge is physically bounded by $v(e) \le v_{\max}$, the time required cannot be smaller than straight-line distance divided by $v_{\max}$:

$$\forall n \in V, \quad h(n) \le h^*(n)$$

Theorem (Russell & Norvig): Because $h(n)$ never overestimates the true remaining transit time, tree search with $A^*$ is guaranteed to return the mathematically optimal shortest-time trajectory.

INCREMENTAL REPLANNING

⚡ Dynamic Search: D* Lite on Traffic Jams

  • Congestion Trigger: When downstream road edge delay inflates by $\Delta t_{\text{edge}} > 25\%$ (e.g. accident or traffic surge), dynamic replanning is triggered.
  • Incremental Tree Repair: Rather than re-running $A^*$ from scratch, D* Lite reverses search direction from goal to vehicle and updates only modified graph nodes in $\mathcal{O}(k \log k)$ runtime ($< 5$ ms).
SYSTEM OBJECTIVE FUNCTION

📐 Global Multi-Criteria Optimization Objective

$$\min J = \sum_{i} \left[ w_1 T_{\text{resp}}(i,a) + w_2 T_{\text{transit}}(a,h) + w_3 T_{\text{handover}}(h) + w_4 \text{Penalty}(i,h) \right]$$
  • $T_{\text{resp}}(i, a)$: Response latency from emergency 911 call to ambulance arrival at scene.
  • $T_{\text{transit}}(a, h)$: En-route travel duration from scene to destination hospital $h$.
  • $T_{\text{handover}}(h)$: Anticipated offload delay based on current ER bay queue.
  • $\text{Penalty}(i, h)$: Clinical mismatch penalty ($\infty$ if hospital lacks Cath Lab / Trauma Level 1).
CONSTRAINTS: $\sum_a y_{ia} = 1 \quad \forall i$ (Every call served) • $\sum_i x_{ih} \le \text{Capacity}_h \quad \forall h$ (Bed limits)
EQUILIBRIUM ALLOCATION

🤝 Two-Sided Stable Matching (Gale-Shapley Adaptation)

Eliminates the dangerous "herd effect" where all ambulances blindly rush to the single closest hospital, causing severe offload delay.

Ambulance Preference: $$\text{Rank}_{\text{amb}}(h) = f(\text{Distance}, \text{WaitTime}, \text{CathLab})$$ Prioritizes ERs with immediate open resuscitation bays.
Hospital Preference: $$\text{Rank}_{\text{hosp}}(a) = f(\text{ESI Acuity}, \text{ETA}, \text{BedReadiness})$$ Hospitals prioritize code-red ESI-1 patients over stable cases.
Stability Guarantee: Mathematically guarantees no blocking pairs $(\text{amb}, \text{hosp})$ exist that mutually prefer each other over their assigned match. Converges in polynomial time $\mathcal{O}(N \times M)$.

🛡 Key Examiner Cross-Examination Defenses

Q1: Why a Multi-Agent System (MAS) rather than a centralized Integer Linear Program?
Examiner Defense: Centralized solvers face combinatorial explosion $\mathcal{O}(n!)$ during urban disasters, creating severe dispatch latency. Furthermore, a central dispatch server is a Single Point of Failure (SPoF). Our MAS decomposes allocation into localized parallel auctions with $\mathcal{O}(k)$ message complexity and seamless edge resilience.
Q2: What is Ambulance Offload Delay (AOD) and how does AURA-EMS prevent it?
Examiner Defense: AOD occurs when an ambulance waits up to 90 minutes outside an emergency department because all triage beds are saturated, immobilizing emergency units. AURA-EMS executes bilateral pre-arrival bed reservation locks via Gale-Shapley matching before vehicle departure, eliminating handover delay.
Q3: What makes the environment "Partially Observable"?
Examiner Defense: No single agent sees global ground truth: ambulances cannot see traffic jams on distant roads until monitored; hospitals do not see arriving patients until alert; dispatchers see coordinates but not internal patient clinical deterioration until paramedics reach the scene.
Q4: Why is A* admissible and guaranteed optimal in your routing engine?
Examiner Defense: Our heuristic $h(n) = \frac{\text{EuclideanDist}(n, \text{Goal})}{v_{\max}}$ uses the theoretical speed limit $v_{\max}$. Since real traffic speed $v(e) \le v_{\max}$, $h(n) \le h^*(n)$ holds universally, strictly guaranteeing optimal shortest-time paths without overestimation.
